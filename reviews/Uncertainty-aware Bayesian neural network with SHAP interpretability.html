Uncertainty-aware Bayesian neural network with SHAP interpretability for data-driven assembly quality prediction in complex manufacturing systems

Uncertainty quantification
Explainable AI
Bayesian neural network
Manufacturing quality prediction
SHAP
Review of an Advanced Engineering Informatics paper combining a Bayesian neural network, uncertainty quantification, and SHAP for interpretable assembly quality prediction in diesel engine manufacturing.
Published

August 5, 2025

  • Authors & Year: Jie Wu, Yan-Ning Sun, Yi-Tian Song, Li-Lan Liu, Zeng-Gui Gao, and Wei Qin (2025)
  • Venue (Journal/Conference): Advanced Engineering Informatics
  • DOI: 10.1016/j.aei.2025.103730
  • Application Domain: Complex manufacturing systems, specifically diesel engine assembly quality prediction
  • Core Model: Uncertainty-aware Bayesian neural network (UABNN) with SHAP interpretability
  • Prediction Target: Diesel engine output power under standard operating conditions
  • Code/Data Available?: Not explicitly stated in the paper
  • Proximity/Threat Level: Medium to High / Related methodological competition if your work involves uncertainty-aware interpretable industrial prediction
  • Date Reviewed: 2026-03-13

2. Core Contributions, Novelty & Motivation

  • The Motivation (Why this problem matters):
    • Complex assembly systems such as diesel engines, CNC machine tools, and aircraft assembly are dynamic, multimodal, and data-imperfect environments.
    • Small upstream deviations accumulate across stations and eventually affect final product quality.
    • Traditional machine learning methods are more transparent, but struggle with multimodal complexity.
    • Deep learning improves accuracy, but sacrifices interpretability and trustworthy uncertainty estimation.
  • The Motivation (Why uncertainty-aware modeling?):
    • The paper argues that industrial assembly data contains three distinct uncertainty sources:
      • Epistemic uncertainty: due to limited knowledge, sparse data, missing conditions, or imperfect model structure.
      • Aleatoric uncertainty: due to inherent noise and randomness in the assembly process.
      • Out-of-distribution (OOD) uncertainty: due to shifts in operating conditions, environmental changes, or unseen inputs.
    • In an industrial setting, simply predicting a point value is not enough. Engineers need to know how reliable that prediction is, and why the model is uncertain.
  • The Motivation (Why explainability?):
    • Even though Bayesian neural networks provide some intrinsic interpretability through probabilistic parameter modeling, the authors argue that this alone is insufficient for complex assembly data.
    • They therefore add SHAP as a post-hoc explanatory layer to:
      • rank influential process variables,
      • support global sensitivity analysis,
      • support localized diagnosis of defective or risky cases,
      • and connect uncertainty intervals to feature-level reasoning.
  • Their claim to fame / Novelty:
    • They propose an integrated framework that combines:
      • a Bayesian neural network,
      • hierarchical priors for epistemic uncertainty,
      • stochastic residual modeling for aleatoric uncertainty,
      • feature perturbation analysis for OOD uncertainty,
      • and SHAP for feature attribution.
    • The paper’s real contribution is not merely “BNN + SHAP,” but rather the attempt to build a unified interpretable uncertainty-aware pipeline for industrial quality prediction.
  • The Value Derived from Explainability (What did XAI actually do?):
    • SHAP was used to identify the most influential assembly and test-bench variables.
    • It provided both:
      • global explanations via feature ranking, and
      • local explanations via interaction analysis between variables.
    • This allowed the model to move beyond prediction and toward practical engineering diagnosis, for example identifying which parameters to inspect or compensate when a product is likely to fail.
  • Their ultimate result:
    • The proposed UABNN outperformed ResNet, VAE, and ViT on the diesel engine dataset:
      • MAE: 0.3419
      • RMSE: 0.4466
      • MAPE: 0.0151
      • R²: 0.9831
    • It also showed better uncertainty behavior through narrower and more stable confidence intervals and achieved the best reported OOD AUROC in the ablation study.

3. Methodology & Technical Details

  • Overall framing:
    • The paper formulates final assembly quality as the cumulative effect of process quality across multiple stations.
    • In the diesel engine case, deviations in variables such as protrusion heights, torque, and axial clearance propagate to downstream test outcomes such as oil pressure, fuel consumption, and torque fluctuation.
  • Core model architecture:
    • The UABNN takes a probabilistic view of network weights.
    • Input dimension: 39
    • Hidden layers: 3
    • Hidden units per layer: 40
    • Output dimension: 1
    • Output target: predicted diesel engine power
  • How uncertainty is modeled:
    • Epistemic uncertainty
      • Captured using hierarchical parameter priors over network weights.
      • This is meant to represent uncertainty due to incomplete knowledge or limited data.
    • Aleatoric uncertainty
      • Captured by explicitly modeling stochastic residual behavior.
      • This is meant to reflect irreducible randomness in the data-generating process.
    • OOD uncertainty
      • Captured through feature perturbation analysis.
      • Perturbed inputs are passed through sampled model configurations, and deviations are used to approximate whether the sample falls outside the training distribution.
  • Bayesian learning details:
    • A standard normal prior is imposed on weights.
    • The posterior is approximated with learnable mean and variance parameters.
    • Variational inference is used.
    • KL divergence regularization is used and its weight is adjusted in stages during training.
    • Adam optimizer is used with an initial learning rate of 0.001.
  • Confidence intervals:
    • The model outputs both a point estimate and a predictive distribution.
    • Confidence intervals are derived from predictive mean and variance.
    • The paper visualizes intervals using up to three standard deviations.
  • How SHAP is used:
    • SHAP values are computed per sample.
    • Global importance is obtained through average absolute SHAP values.
    • Local feature interactions are examined to understand coupling between process variables.
    • The authors position SHAP as a bridge between uncertainty outputs and interpretable engineering diagnosis.

4. Datasets / Experimental Testbed

  • Industrial case study:
    • Real manufacturing data from 1,763 diesel engines produced by a major diesel engine enterprise.
    • The process includes:
      • sub-assembly lines 1–5,
      • the main diesel engine assembly line,
      • a diesel engine performance test line,
      • and a packaging line.
  • Raw data characteristics:
    • The paper mentions 172 assembly characteristic parameters in the full assembly line data.
    • The final UABNN uses 39 input variables and predicts power as the target.
    • Variables include:
      • cylinder liner protrusion heights,
      • piston protrusion heights,
      • torque-related measures,
      • axial clearance,
      • oil pressure,
      • humidity,
      • inlet/outlet temperatures,
      • fuel consumption,
      • intercooler variables,
      • and speed.
  • Data preprocessing:
    • Abnormal data points were deleted.
    • Duplicate data was removed.
    • Continuous missing data was deleted.
    • Small amounts of missing data were filled using mean imputation.
    • The authors also mention statistical preprocessing such as computing mean, standard deviation, and range.

5. Baselines Compared Against

  • ResNet
    • Used as a strong nonlinear predictive baseline for structured industrial data.
    • Configured with three residual blocks, each with 100 hidden units, ReLU, and a fully connected layer.
  • VAE
    • Included because of its relevance to probabilistic modeling and high-dimensional structured data.
    • Configured with a two-layer encoder, each fully connected layer containing 100 hidden units.
  • ViT
    • Included because of its ability to model relationships in high-dimensional tabular data.
    • Configured with:
      • a linear embedding layer,
      • two Transformer encoder layers,
      • four attention heads per layer,
      • and a fully connected regression head.
  • Observation on the baseline set:
    • These are all deep learning baselines, but they are not necessarily the strongest uncertainty-calibrated probabilistic baselines available.
    • The comparison is more about predictive competitors than about a full uncertainty quantification benchmark.

6. Validation Strategy (Predictions, Uncertainty & Explanations)

  • Train/test split:
    • Random split in a 3:2 ratio
    • 60% training, 40% testing
  • Robustness protocol:
    • Experiments were repeated across five random seeds
    • Final metrics reported as mean ± standard deviation
  • Prediction metrics:
    • MAE
    • RMSE
    • MAPE
    • R²
  • Uncertainty stress testing:
    • Gaussian white noise was added to the test set at:
      • 0 dB
      • 3 dB
      • 6 dB
    • This was used to test how confidence intervals and uncertainty distributions behave under increasingly noisy environments.
  • Uncertainty evaluation metrics:
    • ECE for calibration quality
    • Sharpness for concentration of predictive intervals/distributions
    • OOD AUROC for out-of-distribution detection capability
  • XAI validation strategy:
    • Like many SHAP papers, the validation is largely engineering-plausibility based rather than formally quantitative.
    • The authors validate explanations by showing that the top-ranked variables and interactions align with real production experience.

7. Main Results

7.1 Predictive performance

  • UABNN
    • MAE = 0.3419 ± 0.4172
    • RMSE = 0.4466 ± 0.0427
    • MAPE = 0.0151 ± 0.0049
    • R² = 0.9831 ± 0.0010
  • ResNet
    • MAE = 0.4035 ± 0.2179
    • RMSE = 0.5387 ± 0.0523
    • MAPE = 0.0267 ± 0.0072
    • R² = 0.9633 ± 0.0019
  • VAE
    • MAE = 0.7717 ± 0.5347
    • RMSE = 0.8878 ± 0.1278
    • MAPE = 0.0405 ± 0.0146
    • R² = 0.9248 ± 0.0040
  • ViT
    • MAE = 0.5379 ± 0.1473
    • RMSE = 0.6741 ± 0.0695
    • MAPE = 0.0325 ± 0.0085
    • R² = 0.9432 ± 0.0023

7.2 Qualitative prediction behavior

  • The paper shows that UABNN tracks the true power curve more closely than the baselines.
  • The scatter plots are tighter around the ( y=x ) line.
  • Error density plots are more concentrated around zero, suggesting better local accuracy.

7.3 Confidence intervals and uncertainty behavior

  • UABNN produced more stable confidence intervals than ResNet, VAE, and ViT.
  • Under increasing noise, OOD uncertainty increases noticeably.
  • Aleatoric uncertainty also increases with noise.
  • The authors interpret this as evidence that the model distinguishes different uncertainty sources rather than collapsing everything into one generic error bar.

7.4 Ablation study on uncertainty modeling

  • BNN
    • ECE = 0.231
    • Sharpness = 0.029
    • OOD AUROC = 0.51
  • Hierarchical priors only
    • ECE = 0.534
    • Sharpness = 0.021
    • OOD AUROC = 0.48
  • Feature perturbation only
    • ECE = 0.187
    • Sharpness = 0.023
    • OOD AUROC = 0.70
  • Full UABNN
    • ECE = 0.613
    • Sharpness = 0.017
    • OOD AUROC = 0.85
  • Interpretive caution:
    • The OOD AUROC result is easy to interpret: the full method is best.
    • The ECE table is less straightforward, because larger ECE is usually worse in standard calibration terminology.
    • The paper treats the reported values as improvements, so either:
      • they are using an unconventional formulation,
      • or the table/interpretation may contain a definitional inconsistency.
    • This is something worth flagging critically in a review.

8. SHAP Findings & Interpretability Results

  • Top global feature:
    • Cumulative fuel (gas) consumption dominated the SHAP rankings by a large margin.
  • Other important features included:
    • oil pressure,
    • fuel consumption,
    • inlet water temperature,
    • piston protrusion height 06,
    • cylinder liner protrusion height 02,
    • atmospheric humidity,
    • torque,
    • intercooler front temperature,
    • cylinder liner protrusion height 04.
  • What SHAP added:
    • It identified which variables most influenced predicted power.
    • It highlighted how some variables had linear effects and others interacted nonlinearly.
    • It supported diagnostic analysis for when a product is predicted to miss quality standards.
  • Interaction examples discussed by the authors:
    • cumulative fuel consumption with cylinder liner protrusion height 02,
    • oil pressure with cylinder liner protrusion height 05,
    • fuel consumption with piston protrusion height 02.
  • Engineering interpretation:
    • These interactions are framed as meaningful because geometric variables such as protrusion heights affect combustion-related behavior, which then changes how fuel and pressure influence final power output.

9. My Thoughts, Criticisms & Unstated Limitations

  • Criticism 1: The uncertainty taxonomy is good, but the quantification formulas are shaky.
    • The conceptual split into epistemic, aleatoric, and OOD uncertainty is sensible and aligns with modern uncertainty literature.
    • However, the actual equations used to compute these quantities are not especially convincing.
    • The reported formulations look partly heuristic and partly entropy-like, rather than derived from a clearly principled probabilistic decomposition.
    • So while the framework is attractive conceptually, the rigor of the uncertainty definitions seems weaker than the paper’s framing suggests.
  • Criticism 2: The ECE interpretation is potentially problematic.
    • In standard calibration literature, lower ECE is better.
    • Yet the ablation table appears to treat larger ECE as an improvement.
    • That suggests either a notation issue, a reporting error, or a custom metric definition that was not explained clearly enough.
    • This weakens confidence in the uncertainty-validation section.
  • Criticism 3: The comparison set is not a full uncertainty benchmark.
    • ResNet, VAE, and ViT are reasonable predictive baselines, but not the strongest baselines for calibrated uncertainty estimation.
    • Missing comparators include:
      • deep ensembles,
      • MC dropout,
      • heteroscedastic regression networks,
      • quantile regression neural networks,
      • conformal prediction methods,
      • and stronger probabilistic transformers or evidential models.
    • So the paper shows superiority over generic DL baselines, but not necessarily over the state of the art in uncertainty-aware prediction.
  • Criticism 4: SHAP is used well for explanation, but explanation quality is not rigorously evaluated.
    • The paper presents plausible feature rankings and interactions.
    • However, it does not test explanation robustness, stability, or faithfulness.
    • There is no perturbation-based fidelity test, no sanity check against model randomization, and no counterfactual validation.
    • So the SHAP analysis is useful, but still mostly qualitative.
  • Criticism 5: The target seems heavily tied to test-bench variables.
    • Many high-importance features are closely linked to downstream performance measurements rather than purely upstream assembly factors.
    • That raises a practical question:
      • Is the model truly predicting final quality early enough to be useful for intervention,
      • or is it partly reconstructing quality from variables measured close to the target stage?
    • This affects how operationally valuable the model is for proactive quality control.
  • Criticism 6: The industrial realism is good, but generalization is narrow.
    • The paper validates on one manufacturing context: diesel engine assembly.
    • That is valuable, but it does not establish transferability to other complex assembly environments.
    • The authors acknowledge this and mention future work on antenna assembly.
  • Criticism 7: There is a tension between “intrinsic interpretability” and actual interpretability.
    • The paper sometimes overstates the intrinsic interpretability of BNNs.
    • Bayesian parameter distributions improve uncertainty awareness, but that does not automatically make the model human-interpretable in a meaningful operational sense.
    • In practice, most of the usable interpretability here still comes from SHAP, not from the BNN alone.
  • Observation on practical merit:
    • Despite the criticisms, this is a solid applied paper.
    • It is especially useful because it tries to combine:
      • industrial data realism,
      • uncertainty awareness,
      • and feature-level interpretability
    • into one workflow that engineers could plausibly use.

10. What This Paper Contributes to the Literature

  • It strengthens the trend of moving from pure point prediction toward trustworthy prediction in industrial AI.
  • It demonstrates that uncertainty quantification and explainability can be combined in an applied manufacturing setting.
  • It shows that industrial quality prediction papers are starting to move beyond “best RMSE wins” and toward reliability-aware decision support.
  • It also highlights an important gap in the field:
    • many papers now claim uncertainty awareness,
    • but fewer papers rigorously validate whether that uncertainty is well calibrated or operationally meaningful.

11. Relevance to My Own Research / Integration Value

  • Why this paper is useful:
    • It is a strong example of how to frame uncertainty and interpretability together in an applied engineering problem.
    • It provides language and structure for discussing:
      • multiple uncertainty types,
      • interpretability as a practical requirement,
      • and the trade-off between performance and transparency.
  • What I would borrow:
    • The framing that black-box accuracy alone is insufficient in safety-critical or industrial decision systems.
    • The three-part uncertainty decomposition as a motivating structure.
    • The use of SHAP not only for ranking features but also for analyzing feature interactions and diagnosing risky samples.
  • What I would improve:
    • Use stronger probabilistic and uncertainty baselines.
    • Add explicit calibration plots and clearer calibration metrics.
    • Quantitatively validate explanations.
    • Distinguish more carefully between upstream process control variables and downstream proxy measurements.

13. Bottom-Line Verdict

This is a strong applied interpretability-and-uncertainty paper for manufacturing quality prediction. Its biggest strength is the integrated framing: Bayesian prediction, uncertainty decomposition, confidence intervals, and SHAP-based diagnosis all live inside one industrial workflow. Its main weakness is that the uncertainty quantification and explanation validation are not as rigorous as the narrative suggests. So I would treat it as an important application paper with useful ideas and solid empirical value, but not as the final word on principled uncertainty estimation or explanation validation.
