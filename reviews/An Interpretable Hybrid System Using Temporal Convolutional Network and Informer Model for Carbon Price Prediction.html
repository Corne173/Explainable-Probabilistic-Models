Review of a 2025 Systems paper proposing a Boruta-BiTCN-Informer framework with SHAP interpretability and Monte Carlo dropout for interval carbon price forecasting.

1. Quick Reference & Meta-Data

  • Authors & Year: Pei Du, Xuankai Zhang, Tingting Chen, and Wendong Yang (2025)
  • Venue (Journal/Conference): Systems (MDPI)
  • DOI: 10.3390/systems13111011
  • Paper Title: An Interpretable Hybrid System Using Temporal Convolutional Network and Informer Model for Carbon Price Prediction
  • Primary Topic/Concept: Hybrid deep learning for carbon price forecasting with feature selection, interpretability, and interval prediction
  • Core Methods: Boruta, BiTCN, Informer, SHAP, Monte Carlo Dropout
  • Markets Studied: Guangdong and Shanghai carbon markets, with additional robustness tests on EU carbon prices and U.S. Henry Hub natural gas
  • Date Reviewed: 2026-03-13
  • Proximity/Threat Level: Moderate to High, especially if your work also combines multivariate hybrid forecasting with explainability and uncertainty quantification

2. Core Contributions, Novelty & Motivation

The Motivation (Why this problem matters)

Carbon price forecasting matters because carbon prices sit at the center of emissions trading systems and affect both policy design and enterprise compliance decisions. The authors argue that the carbon market is nonlinear, complex, and influenced by many external drivers, so simple historical or deterministic forecasting approaches are inadequate.

The Motivation (Why a hybrid model?)

The paper argues that a single model is often not flexible enough to capture all the components of carbon price dynamics. Carbon prices are influenced by multiple heterogeneous drivers, and different architectures may be better at extracting different structures in the data. Their proposed solution is therefore a staged hybrid architecture: - BiTCN for local spatio-temporal dependence and bidirectional feature extraction - Informer for long-sequence global dependency modeling using sparse attention

The Motivation (Why explainability?)

The paper is explicitly positioned against the black-box nature of many deep forecasting systems. The authors want not only better prediction accuracy, but also: - insight into which drivers matter most, - understanding of regional heterogeneity across markets, - and a more decision-useful model for governments and firms.

The Motivation (Why interval prediction?)

The authors make the very sensible point that point predictions alone are often insufficient in real decision settings. Decision-makers need uncertainty bounds, not just a single predicted value. They therefore add Monte Carlo dropout to obtain prediction intervals.

Their claim to fame / novelty

The paper’s novelty is not a single new algorithm, but rather the integration of several components into one interpretable system: 1. Multi-source feature integration, including structured and unstructured variables, 2. Boruta-based feature selection over 21 candidate factors, 3. BiTCN-Informer hybridization for staged feature extraction, 4. SHAP-based interpretability for both global and local driver analysis, 5. Monte Carlo dropout for interval prediction, 6. Cross-market validation beyond the two core Chinese datasets.

What the XAI actually did

SHAP was used not just decoratively, but to produce market-specific driver rankings and regional comparisons: - In Guangdong, the EU-ETS carbon price was the most influential driver. - In Shanghai, energy and financial variables such as crude oil, natural gas, and USD/CNY were more dominant.

This is important because the explainability output is tied directly to an economic interpretation rather than presented as generic feature-importance plots.

3. Methodology & Technical Details

Overall pipeline

The framework has four major stages: 1. Data collection 2. Feature selection with Boruta 3. Hybrid model construction 4. Further validation and application, including DM tests, extra datasets, SHAP, and interval prediction

Data

The main empirical datasets are: - Guangdong carbon market (GD) - Shanghai carbon market (SH)

Both span: - 1 August 2017 to 31 March 2024 - first 80% used for training, - remaining 20% used for testing.

Candidate features

The study begins with 21 candidate variables across five dimensions:

Environmental indicators

  • Temperature
  • AQI
  • PM2.5
  • PM10

Energy markets

  • EU-ETS carbon price
  • International natural gas price
  • International crude oil price
  • International gasoline price

Financial market indicators

  • China Treasury Bond 1-year
  • China Treasury Bond 5-year
  • China Treasury Bond 10-year
  • USD/CNY
  • EUR/CNY
  • Shanghai Stock Exchange Index
  • Shanghai Stock Exchange Energy Sector Index
  • CSI1000
  • S&P500
  • HS300
  • DJI

Geopolitical risk

  • Geopolitical Risk Index

Unstructured data

  • Baidu Index, obtained from 30 carbon-related keywords and reduced via PCA into a daily time series

Feature selection

Boruta is used as a wrapper feature-selection method around random forests. The key logic is: - create shadow features by shuffling originals, - compare real feature importance against shadow features, - iteratively classify features as important or unimportant.

After Boruta: - 16 important features are retained for each dataset-plus-price-series input setup. - Some features are rejected in both markets, especially AQI, PM2.5, and PM10. - There is market heterogeneity in selected variables: - TEM is confirmed in GD but rejected in SH, - CTB1 is confirmed in GD but rejected in SH, - GPRI and BDI are rejected in GD but confirmed in SH.

This is already an interesting substantive finding, because it suggests the relevance of exogenous factors depends strongly on market structure.

Model architecture

The proposed model is Boruta-BiTCN-Informer.

BiTCN component

BiTCN introduces: - forward and backward dilated convolutions, - a feature fusion layer, - residual connections.

Its purpose is to capture local bidirectional temporal dependencies and reduce feature loss.

Informer component

Informer is used because standard Transformer attention has quadratic complexity. Informer improves this with: - ProbSparse self-attention - self-attention distilling - non-autoregressive generative decoder

Its purpose is to capture global long-range dependencies efficiently.

Hybrid connection mechanism

The output of BiTCN is linearly projected to Informer’s input dimension and then fused with Informer features. Conceptually, this is a staged extraction pipeline: - BiTCN does local/bidirectional feature mining first, - Informer then performs long-range global modeling on these enriched features.

Interpretability

The model is interpreted with SHAP, using Shapley values to estimate the average marginal contribution of each feature.

Uncertainty quantification

For interval prediction, the paper uses Monte Carlo dropout: - dropout remains active at inference, - multiple stochastic forward passes approximate a predictive distribution, - mean and variance across samples define the interval.

The interval is then given by:

with ( z = 1.96 ) for a 95% confidence level.

Hyperparameters

The paper reports: - kernel size = 3 - channels = [64, 64] - dropout = 0.1 - encoder layers = 2 - decoder layers = 1 - dmodel = 4 - feedforward = 64 - multi-heads = 4 - activation = GELU - optimizer = Adam - learning rate = 0.0005 - epochs = 100 - batch size = 64

4. Validation Strategy (Predictions, Uncertainty, and Statistical Testing)

Point prediction metrics

The point forecasts are evaluated using: - MAE - MAPE - RMSE - TIC (Theil inequality coefficient)

Interval prediction metrics

The interval forecasts are evaluated using: - PICP: Prediction Interval Coverage Probability - PINAW: Prediction Interval Normalized Average Width - CWC: Coverage Width-Based Criterion

This is a decent set of interval metrics because it captures both: - whether intervals contain the truth enough times, - and whether those intervals are too wide.

Experimental design

The study runs three major point-prediction experiments:

Experiment I

Compares standard models with: - all variables, - versus Boruta-selected variables.

This tests whether feature selection actually helps.

Experiment II

Compares Boruta-based single models: - LSTM - GRU - TCN - BiLSTM - BiGRU - BiTCN - Transformer - Informer

This identifies the strongest single model.

Experiment III

Builds hybrid models by combining bidirectional architectures with Informer or Transformer: - BiLSTM-Informer - BiGRU-Informer - BiTCN-Informer - BiLSTM-Transformer - BiGRU-Transformer - BiTCN-Transformer

This tests whether staged hybridization improves performance further.

Statistical validation

The paper uses the Diebold-Mariano (DM) test to check whether the forecast improvements are statistically significant.

Robustness validation

The proposed model is also tested on: - EU carbon trading price - Henry Hub natural gas price

This is meant to show broader applicability beyond the two main Chinese carbon markets.

XAI validation

The paper does not use formal explanation evaluation metrics such as: - fidelity, - stability, - robustness, - infidelity, - or sensitivity.

Instead, SHAP is validated through economic plausibility and interpretive usefulness.

5. Results Summary

5.1 Experiment I: Does Boruta help?

Yes, mostly.

For the GD dataset, Boruta improves nearly every benchmark model relative to using all variables.
For example: - ALL-LSTM: MAE = 2.4261 - Boruta-LSTM: MAE = 1.9362

The improvement is especially strong for: - Transformer - ALL-Transformer: MAE = 1.8817 - Boruta-Transformer: MAE = 1.3523

For the SH dataset, Boruta again improves most models: - ALL-LSTM: MAE = 2.3988 - Boruta-LSTM: MAE = 2.0081

This supports the authors’ claim that redundant features interfere with learning and that Boruta improves accuracy by removing weakly informative variables.

Main takeaway

Feature selection is not just preprocessing here; it materially improves performance and likely reduces noise in a high-dimensional multivariate forecasting setup.

5.2 Experiment II: Which single model works best?

Among the Boruta-based single models, Informer performs best overall.

Guangdong

  • Boruta-Informer
    • MAE = 1.3171
    • RMSE = 1.7904
    • MAPE = 0.0352
    • TIC = 0.0181

It outperforms: - Boruta-LSTM - Boruta-GRU - Boruta-TCN - Boruta-BiLSTM - Boruta-BiGRU - Boruta-BiTCN - Boruta-Transformer

Shanghai

  • Boruta-Informer
    • MAE = 1.7424
    • RMSE = 2.5141
    • MAPE = 0.0365
    • TIC = 0.0256

The runner-up is generally Boruta-BiTCN.

Bidirectional effect

The paper also shows that bidirectional variants often outperform their unidirectional counterparts. That is plausible because carbon price sequences likely benefit from richer contextual extraction.

Main takeaway

Before hybridization, Informer is the strongest single architecture, and BiTCN is the strongest local/bidirectional complement. This sets up the logic for combining them.

5.3 Experiment III: Do hybrid models improve performance?

Yes, clearly.

The proposed Boruta-BiTCN-Informer is the best overall model.

Guangdong

  • Boruta-BiTCN-Informer
    • MAE = 0.9642
    • RMSE = 1.3788
    • MAPE = 0.0284
    • TIC = 0.0142

Compare this to: - Boruta-Informer - MAE = 1.3171 - RMSE = 1.7904 - MAPE = 0.0352 - TIC = 0.0182 - Boruta-BiTCN - MAE = 1.5041 - RMSE = 1.9718 - MAPE = 0.0431 - TIC = 0.0201

Shanghai

  • Boruta-BiTCN-Informer
    • MAE = 1.4213
    • RMSE = 2.0154
    • MAPE = 0.0320
    • TIC = 0.0204

Again, this beats both: - Boruta-Informer - Boruta-BiTCN

Informer hybrids vs Transformer hybrids

Across matched hybrid pairings, the Informer-based hybrids generally outperform Transformer-based hybrids.

Main takeaway

The results support the intended architectural story: - BiTCN adds useful local bidirectional structure extraction, - Informer handles long-range dependencies better than Transformer in this setup, - their combination is stronger than either alone.

5.4 Interval prediction results

The interval prediction results are actually quite strong on paper.

Guangdong

  • 95% level: PICP = 0.9693, PINAW = 0.0918
  • 90% level: PICP = 0.9201, PINAW = 0.0922
  • 85% level: PICP = 0.8775, PINAW = 0.0830

Shanghai

  • 95% level: PICP = 0.9553, PINAW = 0.1252
  • 90% level: PICP = 0.9137, PINAW = 0.0905
  • 85% level: PICP = 0.8690, PINAW = 0.0856

The reported PICPs exceed the nominal confidence levels while keeping PINAW relatively low, which the authors interpret as evidence of effective interval prediction.

Main takeaway

The uncertainty module appears practically useful, at least under the paper’s own evaluation framework.

5.5 DM test results

The DM test mostly supports the superiority of the proposed model.

According to the paper: - almost all DM values exceed the 0.05 critical threshold, - most also exceed the 0.01 threshold, - the main exception is Boruta-BiLSTM-Informer on GD, where significance is not established.

Main takeaway

This is a strength of the paper. Many forecasting papers stop at raw metric comparisons. Here, the authors at least attempt statistical confirmation that the gains are not just incidental.

5.6 Robustness on other datasets

The model is additionally tested on: - EU carbon price - Henry Hub natural gas

The paper concludes that the proposed model remains strong in both settings: - in the EU market, it reports an MAE of 1.6336, which is an 11.0240% reduction relative to Boruta-Informer, - in the natural gas market, the MAE improvement over Boruta-Informer is reported as 30.7937%.

Main takeaway

This broadens the paper from being just a two-market case study to a more general multivariate commodity forecasting architecture.

6. Interpretability Findings

Global driver rankings

The SHAP analysis reveals distinct market-specific drivers.

Guangdong

Top drivers include: 1. EU-ETS carbon price 2. China Treasury Bond 10-year 3. International crude oil price 4. China Treasury Bond 5-year 5. Dow Jones Index

The paper argues that Guangdong’s export-oriented industrial base makes it more exposed to EU policy spillovers, especially through mechanisms like the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism.

Shanghai

Top drivers include: 1. International crude oil price 2. International natural gas price 3. USD/CNY 4. S&P 500 5. China Treasury Bond 5-year

The paper interprets this as consistent with Shanghai’s stronger exposure to global financial and energy market dynamics.

Regional heterogeneity

One of the more interesting SHAP findings is that the effect of EUR/CNY differs across markets: - in Shanghai, higher EUR/CNY corresponds to positive SHAP values, - in Guangdong, higher EUR/CNY corresponds to negative SHAP values.

This is one of the better parts of the paper because it shows SHAP being used to uncover heterogeneity, not just to produce a single universal ranking.

Practical recommendations from SHAP

The authors go beyond explanation and suggest decision rules, for example: - Guangdong firms monitoring EUCP SHAP contributions for advance quota procurement, - Shanghai firms monitoring INGP and USD/CNY to inform production and allowance holdings, - regulators assigning different regional weights instead of using uniform national rules.

Main takeaway

SHAP is used in a fairly applied, decision-oriented way. Whether one agrees with all the policy extrapolations, the interpretability section is more substantive than the usual “here is a summary plot” treatment.

7. Strengths

Strength 1: Clear systems-level integration

The study combines: - feature selection, - hybrid deep learning, - interpretability, - and interval prediction

into one coherent pipeline.

Strength 2: Multisource feature design

Using structured and unstructured features is a real strength. The inclusion of the Baidu Index acknowledges that market attention and public sentiment may matter.

Strength 3: Sensible architectural pairing

BiTCN + Informer is not a random mashup. The pairing has a clear rationale: - local bidirectional temporal extraction first, - long-range sparse-attention modeling second.

Strength 4: Strong empirical benchmarking

The study runs multiple experiments, across two main markets and two auxiliary datasets, and includes both metric comparisons and DM tests.

Strength 5: SHAP used for heterogeneity analysis

The interpretability section gives market-specific insights rather than generic feature rankings.

Strength 6: Interval prediction included

This is an important practical addition and makes the study more useful than point-only forecasting papers.

8. Criticisms, Limitations & Unstated Weaknesses

Criticism 1: The paper is still mostly an accuracy paper wearing an interpretability layer

Even though SHAP is used, the core scientific validation remains forecasting accuracy. The interpretability part is meaningful, but it is not rigorously evaluated.

Criticism 2: No formal XAI validation

The paper does not evaluate: - fidelity, - robustness, - sensitivity, - stability, - or consistency of SHAP explanations.

So while the SHAP outputs are plausible, they are not formally validated.

Criticism 3: Monte Carlo dropout is a convenient uncertainty proxy, not deep uncertainty rigor

MC dropout is practical, but it is still an approximation. The paper does not compare it against: - deep ensembles, - conformal prediction, - Bayesian RNN/Transformer variants, - or quantile-based interval methods.

So the uncertainty section is useful, but not especially state-of-the-art.

Criticism 4: No comparison to stronger modern probabilistic baselines

The interval prediction is assessed internally, but there is no head-to-head comparison with: - quantile regression networks, - conformal forecasting, - NGBoost-type probabilistic methods, - distributional deep forecasting models.

That weakens the paper’s claims about uncertainty quantification.

Criticism 5: The hybrid model may be overengineered relative to the evidence

The architecture is more complex than any single baseline, but the paper does not really analyze: - computational cost, - training time, - memory demand, - or deployment practicality.

A production-oriented user would want this.

Criticism 6: No ablation study on SHAP and MC dropout interaction

The paper treats explainability and uncertainty as add-ons after forecasting, but it does not ask whether the uncertainty mechanism affects explanation quality, or whether explanations differ across stochastic forward passes.

Criticism 7: Limited temporal validation design

The data split is an 80/20 train-test split. For financial and market forecasting, a more rigorous rolling-origin or walk-forward evaluation would usually be more convincing.

Criticism 8: Some interpretive claims may be stronger than the evidence supports

SHAP identifies associations in model behavior, not causal effects in the underlying market. Some of the paper’s policy and enterprise recommendations lean a little too close to causal language.

9. My Overall Interpretation

This is a strong engineering-style hybrid forecasting paper with three real contributions: 1. it shows that Boruta helps in a multisource carbon-price setting, 2. it shows that BiTCN + Informer is an effective architectural pairing, 3. it turns the resulting black box into something more usable through SHAP and interval forecasts.

Its biggest weakness is that it presents interpretability and uncertainty quantification as if they are fully validated, when in reality they are only partially demonstrated: - SHAP is economically plausible, but not rigorously tested, - MC dropout intervals look good, but are not benchmarked against stronger UQ alternatives.

So I would describe the paper as: - methodologically solid for applied forecasting, - strong on hybrid modeling and empirical results, - good but not definitive on explainability, - useful but not exhaustive on uncertainty quantification.

11. One-Paragraph Literature Positioning Summary

This paper presents a modern, applied hybrid forecasting framework for carbon price prediction that integrates Boruta-based feature selection, BiTCN local bidirectional feature extraction, Informer long-range sequence modeling, SHAP interpretability, and Monte Carlo dropout interval prediction. Its strongest contribution lies in showing that staged hybrid modeling with carefully filtered multisource features can materially improve carbon price forecasting performance across multiple markets. The SHAP analysis adds real value by revealing region-specific driver heterogeneity, especially the stronger influence of EU-ETS prices in Guangdong and energy-financial variables in Shanghai. However, the paper remains more convincing as an applied forecasting study than as a definitive contribution to explainability or uncertainty quantification, because it does not formally validate explanation quality or compare its uncertainty estimates against stronger contemporary probabilistic baselines.
